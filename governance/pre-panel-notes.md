## What the author expects a cold reader to take away

That each later build could be checked in more ways than the one before,
and that by the last build there were fourteen kinds of check, where the
first had two. Secondarily, that the fourth build was where most of the new
kinds arrived at once.

## Suspected, by part of the chart

**The title and its numbers**
1. "Two ways to check a number" may be read as two columns of the whole
   table rather than the two marks in row one; a reader may look for a
   column named "ways".
2. "Fourteen by build nine" may be read as fourteen marks in row nine. Row
   nine carries ten. A reader who counts row nine will reject the title.
3. A reader may count squares rather than diamonds, reach a number in the
   fifties, and stop trusting the subtitle.
4. Rows seven and eight carry fewer marks than row six. A reader may read
   that as regression and take the title as false, although the title does
   not claim a per-row increase.

**The lane and the other non-build rows**
5. The "Design standard" rows may be read as builds, or as belonging to the
   module row above them.
6. The undated row at the top may be read as "these versions came before
   build one", which the data does not say.
7. The annotation rows sit inside the table and may be read as data rows or
   as footnotes to the row above only.
8. Five version markers in one lane row (v2.3 to v2.7) may read as one long
   event rather than five.

**The headers and labels**
9. The soft-hyphenated header names at the design width ("Inde-pen-dent
   re-deriva-tion") may read as typos.
10. The header numbers may be read as values or counts.
11. Below the breakpoint the header is numbers only; a reader may not find
    the key beneath the table and may take the numbers for scores.
12. The stack phrase in the row label ("Python, DuckDB, dbt, ECharts") may be
    read as a data value or as what the tests are about.
13. The key repeats the header names at the design width and may read as
    clutter, or as a second table.

**Narrow width**
14. At 320 four to five columns are visible beside the sticky labels and the
    only cue to the rest is a shadow. A reader may conclude the later columns
    do not exist, and read every build as carrying at most five surfaces.
15. At 320 the sticky columns take more than half the width; the reader may
    not realise the labels are fixed and the cells move.

**The strip and the prose**
16. "nine Cascadia module repositories on GitHub" may not be recognized as a
    source a reader would name; a reader may ask where the numbers come from.
17. The description under the table is long; a sighted reader who reaches it
    may take it for the finding and not the title.
18. The annotation "the first Python build" names a fact the row label carries
    only as the word "Python" in the stack phrase; a reader may not connect
    them.
